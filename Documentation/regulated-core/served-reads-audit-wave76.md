# Customer-API served-read builder audit — wave 76

- Status: Proposed

Wave 76 line-audits the view BUILDERS behind `packages/customer-api/src/` — the derivation logic that produces the served reads: `activity.mjs`, `kyc.mjs`, `payments.mjs`, `checks.mjs`, `withdrawals.mjs`, `cards.mjs`, `address-screenings.mjs`, `deposits.mjs`, `quotes.mjs`, `capabilities.mjs`, `operations.mjs`. Wave #325 swept all 36 operations at contract level and wave #349 tightened the client parsers to `valid*View` guards; the builders themselves had never been line-audited, and a builder can emit contract-valid-but-wrong records (right shape, wrong values). Each defect below was reproduced as a failing test before the fix landed in the same change.

## Defects found and fixed

1. **Cards reported `expired` with a future expiry and a future last observation.** `buildCard` stamped an already-expired card's `expires_at` at issuance plus one to two years — lands in 2027–2028, ahead of the reader — and `updated_at` equal to that future expiry. The sibling comment described a "shorter past validity window" the code never produced. The builder now derives the expired card's window as one to fourteen days after issuance, fully inside the synthetic epoch's elapsed span, so `expires_at` and its `updated_at` observation are always in the past. In-force cards keep the three-to-four-year window.
2. **QR operations carried an inbound leg on an outbound payment, on any asset.** `buildOperationItem` directed every non-withdrawal single leg `in` and drew the qr asset from the full {RUB, USDT, TON} pool. A qr operation projects the subject's own SBP rail payment: the payments domain settles RUB outbound only, and the miniapp's operation vocabulary renders the leg the same way (`{ asset: "RUB", direction: "out" }`). The builder now draws qr legs from the RUB-only pool directed `out`, and `validOperationView` fails closed on an inbound or non-RUB qr leg — the validator had pinned the same wrong rule the builder emitted. The miniapp's served-view parser carried the same legacy rule (`miniapp/src/server/customer-api-client.ts`): it now serves the corrected qr shape and fails closed on an inbound or non-RUB qr leg, verified by e2e (the wallet surface consults the upstream operations read and degraded when the parser rejected the corrected legs).
3. **Address screenings derived a doubled deadline window.** `DEADLINE_MS` was 1_800_000 while the mirrored surfaces pin 900 seconds — the miniapp's `MINIAPP_KYT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` default and the KYT simulator's `screeningTimeoutSeconds` default are both 900. The served record's `deadline − submitted_at` therefore reported twice the window the surface it mirrors carries. The builder now emits 900_000 and the validator pins the exact window like the quote TTL pin, so a drifted deadline fails closed.

## Verified clean

- **Derivation coherence.** `kyc.mjs` bounds the application status by the session KYC state the capability gate already evaluated (verified ⇒ the completed record, pending ⇒ inside the review window, unverified ⇒ never a submitted or completed record), so the served application can never contradict the gate. `operations.mjs` derives its per-kind status vocabulary from the sibling directories' own status sets rather than re-declaring them; `activity.mjs` does the same across nine domains. The `syntheticActivityItems`/`buildWalletsView` exports keep the operator-side projections on the same builders — the identity-by-construction pattern holds where the same fact appears twice.
- **Lifecycle arithmetic.** `expires_at − created_at` is exactly the custody intent TTL (300 s) on withdrawals; `updated_at` is pinned per status (`draft` untouched, `expired` at expiry, `confirmed` after it, everything else strictly inside the window). Quotes pin `expires − issued == ttl_seconds` with `price_observed_at <= issued_at` and recompute every derived amount simulator-faithfully (same `divideRounded` modes on the same 20_000/10_000 denominators). Checks resolve strictly inside the 72 h window, `expired` exactly at it; deposits pin per-status totals (`received == expected` / `2×` / partial `<` / `0`) and payments pin `total == amount + fee` with provider references exactly when the rail observed the instruction.
- **Ordering.** Every bounded collection builds index-ascending with jitter strictly below the step, then reverses — newest first, strictly decreasing `created_at`, unique ids. `validOperationsView` and `validActivityView` pin the same order plus one entry per kind; screenings keep the allowlisted three targets in submission order, mirroring the miniapp store's Map insertion order.
- **Cross-subject isolation.** Member reads resolve inside the subject's own derived view (`operationFor`, `viewFor`, `orderFor`, `depthFor`, `subjectFor`, `queueItemFor`): a foreign or never-minted id returns the pinned 404 by construction, never a cross-subject record.
- **Enum derivation.** Every emitted status comes from the exported `*_STATUSES` sets that mirror the contract dictionaries and the custody/bank simulator lifecycles verbatim (`unsigned_intent_ready` included); no locally invented status literal exists. Activity's kyc vocabulary is the application states minus `not_started`, and its security vocabulary is the miniapp's login/revoked pair.
- **Capability gating.** Every served route evaluates the same capability before any directory call — verified arm by arm: member reads share their collection's capability, the unconditional reads (session, capabilities, kyc, profile, support, auth, users) stay readable to unverified subjects, and the asset/activity collections gate on verified KYC. The policy dictionary covers every served operation exactly once; denied financial and onboarding capabilities carry their decision reason codes.
- **Bounds and clocks.** All builders are pure functions of the subject (plus the session KYC state for the kyc view) with fixed synthetic epochs — no wall-clock reads, no per-subject memo caches, bounded counts (1–9 items) everywhere. Emission is canonical: `.toISOString()` timestamps that reserialize to themselves and `decimal()` fixed-scale strings.

## Observations

- The `expired`/`expired_no_payment` fiat intents stamp `updated_at` one second past the intent deadline (the observation of the expiry, not the deadline itself), while withdrawals pin `updated_at == expires_at` and checks pin `resolved_at == expires_at`. Each module is internally coherent; the asymmetry is noted rather than fixed.
- A `submitted`/`in_review` KYC application can carry `updated_at` past its `review_deadline` — a breached deadline while still under review. Contract-valid and plausible; no contract pin exists either way.
- Activity detail strings cite domain-shaped ids (`dep_`/`wdr_`/`qte_`/`ord_`/`pay_`/`crd_`/`chk_` + 24 hex) that are freshly derived, not resolved from the sibling collections — a deliberate "reads like" simulation documented in the module header. The one member-read-bearing kind (`ord_`) cites an id that does not resolve through `getCustomerExchangeOrder`; recorded as an observation because no resolvability promise exists in the contract.
- Contract timeline states `pending`/`blocked` are unreachable in the current operation builder (all steps emit `done`/`current` with timestamps); the vocabulary is declared, not exercised.
- Wallet `available`/`hold` and deposit `received_total` are independent derivations with no claimed invariant between them — noted for the record, consistent with their synthetic domains.

## Test additions

Six new cases in `packages/customer-api/tests/served-reads-audit-wave76.test.mjs`, each written to fail on the unfixed builder: the mirrored 900-second screening deadline pin (collection and fail-closed validator arms), the qr leg asset/direction pin plus its validator mirror, the expired-card past-window pin, and a guard that in-force cards keep their forward validity window. One fixture in `tests/address-screenings.test.mjs` carried the drifted 1800-second deadline and was moved to the mirrored window. The verification-integrity baseline gained the new file's pins and the lint script its `node --check` line.
